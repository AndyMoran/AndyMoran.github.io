# Andrew Moran  
Applied Data Scientist & Quantitative Modeller  
Energy Systems · Infrastructure Analytics · Physics‑First Simulation · Decision‑Making Under Uncertainty

I build models that help people make better decisions. I take messy operational data, build reproducible analytical pipelines, and turn it into evidence that can stand up to scrutiny from engineers, traders and regulators.

My current focus is GB electricity markets and energy‑system transition: storage, flexibility, transmission constraints, VPPs, LDES, curtailment, locational value and market‑design incentives. But the underlying methods are broader and grounded: event detection, system‑stress analysis, resource allocation, probabilistic modelling and decision support for complex infrastructure systems.

I’m now looking to bring this approach into analytical, data‑science, consultancy or research roles where complex technical decisions require rigorous evidence, transparent modelling and practical judgement. I’m especially interested in energy systems, infrastructure resilience, emergency‑response analytics, flexibility markets and uncertainty‑led decision support.

My work is open, MIT‑licensed, and documented on GitHub — each project treated as a research product with clear methods, reproducible pipelines, results, charts, interpretation and caveats.

## Current Research Programme

My projects follow a common workflow:

**messy operational data → reproducible pipeline → physical/event model → economic or policy interpretation → uncertainty / sensitivity testing → decision‑ready output**

The energy work currently falls into four connected themes:

### **Long‑duration storage economics**  
How duration, location, technology and financing structure affect support needs.

### **Constraint and balancing‑market analytics**  
How real system stress appears in BM actions, BOALF data and regional dispatch.

### **Distributed flexibility and VPP value**  
Whether domestic batteries are actually available at event time, once consumer constraints are respected.

### **Strategic demand siting**  
How new large loads — including AI infrastructure — should be evaluated against constraint direction, renewable absorption and residual grid burden.
